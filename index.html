Policy Brief: Making Sense Of Varying Policies For Reclassifying English Learners: A PPIC survey of nearly 350 California local education agencies, serving 42% of the state's English Learners, found districts vary widely in their reclassification standards despite nearly all (99%) using the ELPAC for English proficiency testing. Districts use up to 24 different basic-skills tests, with about half applying the stricter SBAC "Standard Met" threshold (which many English-only students fail) and half using lower cutoffs; staff were evenly split on whether the overall process is "too hard" or "just right," with 47% of critics citing ELPAC score barriers and 29% flagging specific challenges for English Learners in special education. The reclassification process consumes over 786,000 staff hours annually statewide; PPIC recommends relying solely on the ELPAC, automating decisions to cut delays, and improving parent communication about the process.

California Public School Enrollment Has Fallen 8% Over The Past Decade, Finds Study: A new PPIC report finds California K-12 enrollment has dropped 8% over the past decade, with another 10% decline projected by 2035, driven primarily by falling birth rates and immigration shifts rather than private/homeschool transitions (which account for under 5% of the loss).

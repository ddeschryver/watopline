This is notable. Remember 2004? Not many people do, but this was big news then, and it's still in play. The Williams Act instructional materials review is an annual compliance process in California designed to ensure that every public school student has sufficient textbooks and instructional materials to participate fully in classroom instruction. The requirement stems from the 2004 settlement of Williams v. California, a lawsuit challenging unequal educational conditions, particularly in schools serving low-income students. At 1:22:27, District Librarian Tasha Weatherall presented the annual Williams Act instructional materials review. All 36 audited schools achieved full compliance, with sufficient textbooks and instructional resources. The board approved Resolution 3607. Weatherall reported, “We did achieve 100% compliance during our site visits.” Approximately $2.8 million in lottery funds supports instructional materials, including upcoming curriculum adoptions.
